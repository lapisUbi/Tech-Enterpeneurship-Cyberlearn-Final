# Staff beta | facilitator and observer sheet

**Purpose:** facilitator protocol and evaluation method. Do not upload names, emails, recordings or signed consent sheets to the public submission. Keep a separately protected record of permission. Use new codes P01–P08. Do not count a repeat visit or a technical QA record as another person.

## Recruit and obtain permission
For a future deployment, plan eight adult university or college employees across teaching, admissions/administration and finance, covering both interface languages. Record eligibility in broad terms. Invite two institutional buyers separately. They cannot stand in for learner usability observations unless independently eligible and performing the learner task.

Read: “We are testing the product, not your performance. Participation is voluntary and you can stop or skip a question. May we keep coded notes about the task and your feedback? We do not need real messages, staff credentials, student data or personal information.” Obtain separate permission for any recording; written observer notes are sufficient. Explain retention, who may see the de-identified notes and how to request removal. Agree an actual removal date with the institution. If permission is declined, do not collect a record. Do not put consent signatures in the submitted CSV.

## During one session
1. Open the release in a new authorised browser profile. Ask the participant to choose a language and desk and complete one shift. Start the clock at the role screen. Do not preselect answers, explain controls or hint which mail is suspicious.
2. Ask the person to make both decisions, read one explanation, finish the final four unseen items and show what each result means. Observe where they hesitate or ask for help. Record exactly what happened before interpreting it.
3. Ask the participant to deliver the result to the facilitator using the agreed method. Offline: export the JSON and share it privately; facilitator imports it. Classroom: observe learner receipt **and** confirm that the reviewer can open the record. A download alone does not prove delivery.
4. Ask “Which example felt closest to your work, and which felt least familiar?” and “On a scale of 1–5, how relevant was this shift to your work?” Missing ratings are missing, not zero. Ask permission before quoting. Do not improve the participant’s answer when recording it.
5. Record participant code, role, language, date, consent reference, elapsed minutes, help, exact observation, issue severity and file reference. Define blocking = cannot reach an outcome independently; major = incorrect or missing handoff; minor = recoverable friction. Link any fix to a build/change and retest, keeping the original observation. Keep technical checks separate from staff observations.

## Interpretation and follow-up
Keep usability, learning and purchase evidence apart. For eight staff the design target is six who **both** finish without help within ten minutes and rate relevance 4/5 or higher. Report the count that meets both conditions and the true denominator. An incomplete or unsuccessful test is an observation, not a reason to replace its result. Flag a repeated blocking problem for repair before a paid pilot.

Record independent-check classification and safest action separately. For each set report threat hit rate as correctly flagged threats / all threats; false-alarm rate as routine messages flagged / all routine messages; safe-action accuracy as best actions / all answered items. A denominator of zero is not applicable. Report raw counts. Guided practice, which provides coaching, does not belong in the independent score. The four-item checks are not calibrated or a validated individual risk instrument.

After seven full days, invite willing participants using the same device and browser profile; do not change the clock. The follow-up draws four separately reserved items. Record linked parent ID, eligible count, number returning, time interval and score. Report missing visits separately. If fewer than seven days have elapsed, report follow-up as pending rather than manufacturing data.

## Buyer discussion (separate register)
Ask about their last training purchase, current licence or internal alternative, who approves a new vendor, security review, existing budget and how records should be delivered. Present the actual modelled offer (600 KZT per seat/month, example 200 seats and 150,000 KZT onboarding). Record accept, decline, counteroffer or no decision; do not interpret interest as a sale. Ask permission for any coded notes. Record an explicit next step, owner and date only if agreed.

## Recording outcomes
The protocol alone does not meet the user-reported final requirement for testing with at least five real people. When the study is carried out, retain coded observations and link each finding to its build and task. Update the plan and portfolio with the actual outcomes. Keep application checks separate. Identifiable information does not belong in the submission pack.


## V13 completion gate and additional observations
At least five distinct real participants must actually use the MVP. Eight relevant staff remain the research target; five is the user-reported submission minimum. V15 contains five supplied completed sessions, with team confirmation; see the updated EvidencePortfolio for observations and limitations. Record all attempted sessions and failures. Use P01-P08 once per person. Keep dates, coded role eligibility, permission, observations, real result JSON and specific feedback. Complete Team_Contribution_Record.csv personally; do not infer shares from proposed responsibilities.

Observer measures: fatigue 1=not tiring to 5=very tiring; start report-decision timer when an unseen threat appears and stop when the person commits the classification; record seconds for each relevant item in coded notes, then the participant median in the register. This is simulated decision latency, not real incident-reporting speed. Record current vs prototype administrative minutes for an equivalent review task, stating the task and workload. No automatic per-item timing or fatigue field is implemented in the app; the observer records them. Derive pre/post counts from real exported answers, excluding coached practice.

Pilot targets are proposed, not observed: post-check threat hit rate >=80%, routine false-alarm rate <=20%, paired safe-action improvement >=20 percentage points; median threat-decision time <=60 seconds; median fatigue <=2/5 with no repeated 4-5 ratings; six of eight complete unaided within ten minutes and relevance >=4/5. With five initial participants report raw counts and describe the eight-person gate as pending. Four-item checks are coarse; report all denominators and ceiling effects. These are formative gates, not validated efficacy thresholds.

Compare 600 and 700 KZT/seat/month with the same support scope, term and onboarding charge. Alternate which offer is shown first and record responses and reasons separately. Two buyer conversations are exploratory, not a market estimate. A written paid-pilot commitment with an authorised buyer is stronger than a positive interview.
