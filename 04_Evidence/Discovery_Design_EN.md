# Customer discovery and evaluation design

The method investigates the fit of short, role-relevant email-safety exercises for educational employers. Learners and budget holders are separate audiences.

## Role coverage

Teaching: document and portal requests. Admissions and administration: applicant records and unexpected attachments. Finance: supplier payment details and independent verification. Library, reception, secretarial, laboratory and canteen roles broaden the discovery questions; relevance should be assessed before extending the three-track MVP.

## Ten discovery questions

1. What cybersecurity training or instructions are normally available at work?
2. Which type of message creates uncertainty about what to do next?
3. Which part of current training is difficult to remember or use?
4. How is an uncertain request checked today?
5. Which explanation format is most useful, and why?
6. Where could brief practical scenarios fit into the working day?
7. What feedback is needed after an unsafe choice?
8. What practice frequency would fit existing responsibilities?
9. What would make the guidance easier to apply later?
10. What would build trust in a new institutional training service?

## Analysis and decision method

Code responses by job context, current workaround, training burden, feedback, cadence, privacy and trust. Retain counterexamples. Separate an expressed preference from observed completion and from a purchasing commitment. Link each proposed change to a question, observation or technical risk, then define the next test before implementing it.

## Buyer track

Identify purchasing authority, staff seat count, current licences, procurement calendar, budget, security review and expected support. Present the modelled 600 KZT per seat per month and 150,000 KZT one-time onboarding with an explicit service scope. Record acceptance, refusal, counteroffer or no decision without treating interest as a sale.

## Evaluation package

Fieldwork_EN.md gives the task script and observation rules. Staff_Test_Register.csv provides fields for eight planned sessions. Portfolio section 3 provides five tasks covering entry, recovery, the complete shift, handoff and follow-up. No achieved task rate or customer commitment is assumed by the method.
