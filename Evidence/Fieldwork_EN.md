# Staff beta | facilitator and observer sheet

**Purpose:** facilitator protocol and evaluation method. Do not upload names, emails, recordings or signed consent sheets to the public submission. Keep a separately protected record of permission. Use new codes P01–P08. Do not count a repeat visit or a technical QA record as another person.

## Recruit and obtain permission
For a future deployment, plan to recruit eight adult university employees from teaching, admissions/administration and finance, covering both interface languages. Record eligibility in broad terms. Invite two institutional buyers separately. They cannot stand in for learner usability observations unless independently eligible and performing the learner task.

Read: “We are testing the product, not your performance. Participation is voluntary and you can stop or skip a question. May we keep coded notes about the task and your feedback? We do not need real messages, staff credentials, student data or personal information.” Obtain separate permission for any recording; written observer notes are sufficient. Explain retention, who may see the de-identified notes and how to request removal. Agree an actual removal date with the institution. If permission is declined, do not collect a record. Do not put consent signatures in the submitted CSV.

## During one session
1. Open the release in a new authorised browser profile. Ask the participant to choose a language and desk and complete one shift. Start the clock at the role screen. Do not preselect answers, explain controls or hint which mail is suspicious.
2. Ask the person to make both decisions, read one explanation, finish the final four unseen items and show what each result means. Observe where they hesitate or ask for help. Record exactly what happened before interpreting it.
3. Ask the participant to deliver the result to the facilitator using the agreed method. Offline: export the JSON and share it privately; facilitator imports it. Classroom: observe learner receipt **and** confirm that the reviewer can open the record. A download alone does not prove delivery.
4. Ask “Which example felt closest to your work, and which felt least familiar?” and “On a scale of 1–5, how relevant was this shift to your work?” Missing ratings are missing, not zero. Ask permission before quoting. Do not improve the participant’s answer when recording it.
5. Record participant code, role, language, date, consent reference, elapsed minutes, help, exact observation, issue severity and file reference. Define blocking = cannot reach an outcome independently; major = incorrect or missing handoff; minor = recoverable friction. Link any fix to a build/change and retest, keeping the original observation. Keep technical checks separate from staff observations.

## Interpretation and follow-up
Keep usability, learning and purchase evidence apart. For eight staff, the design target is six who **both** finish without help within ten minutes and rate relevance 4/5 or higher. Report the count that meets both conditions and the true denominator. Retain incomplete and unsuccessful tests as observations rather than replacing their results. Flag a repeated blocking problem for repair before a paid pilot.

Record independent-check classification and safest action separately. For each set report threat hit rate as correctly flagged threats / all threats; false-alarm rate as routine messages flagged / all routine messages; safe-action accuracy as best actions / all answered items. A denominator of zero is not applicable. Report raw counts. Exclude guided practice from the independent score because it provides coaching. The four-item checks are uncalibrated and have not been validated as an individual risk instrument.

After seven full days, invite willing participants to return using the same device and browser profile. Do not change the clock. The follow-up draws four separately reserved items. Record linked parent ID, eligible count, number returning, time interval and score. Report missing visits separately. If fewer than seven days have elapsed, report follow-up as pending rather than manufacturing data.

## Buyer discussion (separate register)
Ask about their last training purchase, current licence or internal alternative, who approves a new vendor, security review, existing budget and how records should be delivered. Present the actual modelled offer (600 KZT per seat/month, example 200 seats and 150,000 KZT onboarding). Record acceptance, refusal, a counteroffer or no decision. Do not interpret interest as a sale. Ask permission for any coded notes. Record a specific next step, owner and date only if agreed.


## Additional measurements and gates
Ask participants to rate fatigue from 1 (not tiring) to 5 (very tiring). Record assistance and interruptions. If timing a threat decision, begin when the unseen item appears and end when the choice is committed; this is simulated decision latency, not real incident reporting speed. Neither per-item timing nor fatigue is automatically collected by this application. Compare current and prototype administrative minutes only for the same defined task and workload.

Proposed formative targets: six of eight finish unaided within ten minutes AND relevance at least 4/5; paired safe-action improvement at least 20 percentage points; post-check threat detection at least 80%; false alarms at most 20%; median fatigue at most 2/5. None is established by the current five sessions. Four-item checks provide coarse measures. Report raw counts, all attempts and missing outcomes.

Use the same support scope, seat count, term and onboarding charge for 600/700 KZT buyer offers, alternate order and record reasons. Two exploratory discussions cannot establish a demand curve. The buyer register has no completed interviews. Obtain actual permission before collecting or sharing more evidence.
